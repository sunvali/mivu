# Spanish (es) glossary

Written before the translation; every Spanish string follows it. Neutral Latin
American Spanish, for US Hispanic and Latin American readers, shipped as bare
`es` so Spanish phones everywhere get it. Reviewed by agents; no native
speaker has read it yet.

## Voice

- **Register:** tú, never usted, vos or vosotros. Warm, short, everyday.
- **Gender-neutral toward the person.** Spanish marks gender on adjectives and
  participles about "you", so use verbs, nouns or "tu" + noun instead:
  "¿Todo listo?" (not "¿Listo?"), "Te damos la bienvenida" (not
  "Bienvenido"), "¿Quieres confirmarlo?" (not "¿Seguro?"), "Lo lograste",
  "Llevas una racha excelente". No "@", "e", "x" or "/a" forms.
- **Buttons and menu items:** infinitives or nouns, as Apple's Spanish writes
  them: "Guardar", "Cancelar", "Agregar comida".
- **Errors:** "No pudimos …", then what to do: "Inténtalo de nuevo".
- **Pip:** speaks without gendered forms of either Pip or the person; present
  tense or first-person plural ("Ya está", "Veamos").
- **Spain-only words:** avoid móvil, ordenador, zumo, patata, apuntar, coger.
- **Past tense:** the preterite, as in Mexico and the US: "¿Qué comiste hoy?", not "¿Qué has comido?".

## Names

- **Mivu**, **Pip**, ChatGPT, OpenAI, Apple, Apple Intelligence, Siri, USDA and
  iPhone stay as they are.
- **Pip** has no article and no gender: "Pip te ayuda", "Pregúntale a Pip".
  Never "el Pip" / "la Pip". Pip is a fox.
- **Coach** (the tab and the feature) stays "Coach", ungendered.
- **Snap It** stays in English, as an invariable name with no article:
  "Registra tu comida con Snap It".

## Siri phrases

Imperative tú ("Registra una comida en ${applicationName}"). Every shortcut
that names Snap It also has a phrase without the English name, because Spanish
speech recognition may not catch it.

## Plurals

Spanish needs `one`, `many` and `other`; `many` is for millions ("1 millón de"),
so it reads like `other`. Only `one` may drop the number.

## Numbers and dates

The system formats numbers, dates and units from the phone's region (decimal
point in Mexico and the US, comma in Spain). Fixed text never contains a
written-out number format or date; a limit written into a sentence groups thousands with a non-breaking space ("100 000"), which reads in every region. Day before month ("15 de noviembre").
