# Hebrew (he) glossary

Written before the translation; every Hebrew string follows it. The owner
reviews all strings on one page before the language ships.

## Voice

Gender-neutral everywhere, on screens and from Pip. Hebrew marks gender on
present-tense verbs, the future and imperative of "you", and adjectives about
the person, so:

- **Buttons and menu items:** nouns or infinitives. "שמירה", "ביטול",
  "הוספת ארוחה", "לנסות שוב".
- **Instructions and hints:** "אפשר ל…" for a hint, "צריך ל…" for a
  requirement, or the infinitive. "אפשר לנסות שוב", "צריך לאפשר גישה למצלמה
  בהגדרות". Not the formal "יש ל…", "נדרשת", "פירושו".
- **Errors:** "לא הצלחנו ל…", not "לא ניתן (היה) ל…". The plural imperative
  ("נסו", "בחרו") only where nothing else reads naturally.
- **Statements about the person:** past tense, whose spelling doesn't show
  gender ("שמרת", "אכלת"), possessives ("היום שלך", "היעד שלך"), or impersonal
  wording ("נרשמו 1,240 קק״ל"). Never "אתה" / "את", present-tense "you"
  verbs ("אתה אוכל"), or adjectives about the person ("מוכן", "בטוח").
- **Pip:** speaks without gendered forms of either Pip or the person: past
  tense, impersonal or first-person plural ("נרשם", "הכול מוכן", "בואו
  נבדוק"). Warm and short, never formal ("הנך", "אנא").
- **Tone:** plain everyday Hebrew, as an Israeli app would write it. Prefer
  short native words to loanwords or formal register.

## Names

- **Mivu** stays in Latin letters. Other names stay too: ChatGPT, OpenAI,
  Apple, Apple Intelligence, Siri, USDA. iPhone is "אייפון" ("באייפון הזה"),
  as Apple's Hebrew writes it.
- **Pip** is "פיפ", and so is the Coach tab.
- A Hebrew prefix before a Latin word takes an ASCII hyphen, not the maqaf:
  "ל-ChatGPT", "ב-Mivu".
- The same hyphen goes before a placeholder that can start with a digit or a
  Latin letter: "נוסף ל-%@" when %@ may be "15 בנוב׳". No hyphen when it is
  always a Hebrew word ("העברה ל%@" with a day name).

## Siri phrases

The phrase Shortcuts shows is a neutral infinitive ("לשאול את פיפ ב-Mivu").
The list also accepts the masculine and feminine commands ("שאל", "שאלי"), so
people can speak naturally; nothing on screen is gendered.

## Plurals

Hebrew needs `one`, `two` and `other`. `one` and `two` may drop the number
("ארוחה אחת", "יומיים", "שתי ארוחות"); `other` keeps it. Digits stay 0–9.

## Dates

Gregorian, Hebrew month and day names from the system. Day before month
("15 בנובמבר").

## Weak legacy terms not to reuse

From the first Hebrew draft: "יום ערות", "יישום", "נערך על ידיכם", "סכומים
מתועדים", and "מחזור הערות".
